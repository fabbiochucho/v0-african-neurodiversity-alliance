# Adult self-check

`/check/adult` (`lib/adult-check.ts`, `app/check/adult/`). For adults who wonder whether they are neurodivergent and
were never assessed, often prompted in their 30s or 40s by a child's or relative's diagnosis. Free, no sign-in, no AI,
nothing sent or stored.

## What it does

26 statements answered Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Very often, one per screen, with read aloud. Areas:
autism (7), ADHD (6, one about childhood), reading and spelling (4), numbers (3), coordination (3), tics (1), and two
wellbeing questions (low mood, anxiety over the last two weeks) that only signpost.

An area is "worth exploring with a professional" when at least half its statements are answered often or very often.
Either wellbeing statement shows a health-worker and crisis-line message first. The result explains who assesses adults,
what to bring (childhood examples, school reports, someone who knew you as a child), links the directory, ANDA's reviewed
answer on adult diagnosis, and NeuRafiki's adult assessment when federation is on. Printable.

## Sources and licences

ANDA's own wording. The areas follow what adult assessments cover (NICE CG142 for autism in adults, NICE NG87 for ADHD
including childhood onset, ICD-11 6A00-6A05 and 8A05 for the conditions). No statement is copied or reworded from AQ-10,
RAADS-R, CAT-Q, ASRS v1.1, PHQ or GAD, whose licences restrict commercial or digital use or need permission.

## Adapted for the context

Everyday examples from African life (weddings, funerals, church or mosque, sermons, market noise, generators, plaiting
hair, account numbers); "compared with people around you in your own community"; masking named directly, since many
adults, especially women, have learned to hide their difficulties; childhood questions marked, since many adults
can't easily ask a parent.

## Not validated

Same rule as the early check (docs/EARLY_CHECK.md): call it a self-check, never a screening result, until a study
measures it. ponytail: the half-the-area rule is a starting point; tune it with study data.
